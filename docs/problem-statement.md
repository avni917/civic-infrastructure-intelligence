# Problem Statement

## 1. Project Title

**Civic Infrastructure Intelligence — A Civic Decision-Support Platform**

## 2. Background

Urban and semi-urban areas frequently experience infrastructure problems such as potholes, damaged roads, broken streetlights, blocked drains, water leakage, and improper waste disposal.

Citizens may report these issues through municipal portals, mobile applications, or other communication channels. However, collecting reports is only one part of effective civic infrastructure management. Authorities must also understand which reports describe the same physical problem, determine which incidents require urgent attention, identify the responsible department, and coordinate limited repair resources.

Without effective incident analysis and prioritization, repeated reports, delayed responses, and recurring infrastructure problems can make civic issue management more difficult.

## 3. Problem Definition

The project focuses on five related challenges:

1. **Duplicate reporting:** Multiple citizens may report the same physical issue, resulting in repeated records of one incident.
2. **Incident prioritization:** Authorities need a consistent method to distinguish urgent safety-related problems from lower-priority issues.
3. **Department routing:** Reports must reach the appropriate department based on the nature and location of the problem.
4. **Resource coordination:** Repair teams have limited availability, skills, time, and geographical coverage.
5. **Resolution accountability:** A status marked as resolved does not necessarily mean the underlying issue has been permanently fixed.

A reporting system can collect information about civic issues, but additional analysis is needed to turn that information into actionable decisions.

## 4. Proposed Direction

Civic Infrastructure Intelligence aims to develop a web-based decision-support platform that transforms individual citizen reports into organized, trackable infrastructure incidents.

The system will explore duplicate detection, explainable priority scoring, department recommendations, repair-team allocation, geographical visualization, and recurring-issue analysis.

## 5. Intended Impact

The project aims to demonstrate how structured data, algorithms, and software engineering can help authorities:

- Understand the actual number and distribution of reported incidents.
- Identify incidents that may require urgent attention.
- Reduce unnecessary duplication in incident records.
- Coordinate repair resources more systematically.
- Track the progress and history of reported problems.
- Identify areas where similar problems repeatedly occur.

## 6. Project Boundary

The project will initially be developed as an independent educational prototype using sample or synthetic data. It will not directly control municipal operations or replace official reporting and emergency services.

## 7. Central Question

How can citizen-generated civic reports be transformed into reliable, explainable, and actionable information that supports infrastructure incident prioritization, response coordination, and accountability?
